# Case Study 01: The Pull of Visual Defaults

## 1. Overview

**Research area:** Generative AI image creation  
**Researcher:** Ashayaki  
**Status:** Preliminary retrospective observation  
**Evidence level:** Exploratory; controlled validation pending

## 2. Research Question

When a user provides a specific creative direction, do image generators consistently follow the intended concept, or do their outputs sometimes gravitate toward familiar visual patterns and stylistic defaults?

## 3. Background

This question emerged from practical experimentation with multiple AI image-generation tools and repeated attempts to achieve specific visual outcomes.

The work included character creation, visual identity preservation, aesthetic direction, and iterative prompt refinement.

During these experiments, different tools and sessions did not always respond to creative instructions in the same way. Some outputs were visually polished but did not fully match the intended creative direction. Other attempts required additional corrections to preserve important character details.

These experiences motivated a closer examination of recurring model tendencies.

## 4. Preliminary Observations

The following observations are based on retrospective experience rather than a controlled comparison.

### A. Visual polish does not guarantee creative alignment

An output may appear aesthetically pleasing while still missing the requested mood, identity, or artistic intention.

### B. Different generators can produce different interpretations

Similar creative objectives may lead to different visual outcomes across tools. The reasons for these differences have not yet been isolated.

### C. Character details can be difficult to preserve

In character-generation experiments, maintaining specific identity features across outputs sometimes required repeated instructions and corrections.

### D. Iteration can reveal behavioral patterns

Repeated attempts may help distinguish isolated mistakes from tendencies that recur under similar conditions.

However, systematic records are needed to determine how consistent these patterns actually are.

## 5. Current Hypothesis

Some generative image models may favor familiar visual patterns when interpreting creative instructions, particularly when the requested direction conflicts with their learned stylistic tendencies or when instructions leave room for interpretation.

This is a working hypothesis, not an established explanation for the observed outputs.

Other factors, including prompt wording, model version, generation settings, reference images, and randomness, may also contribute.

## 6. Current Limitations

The observations documented here were not collected under a standardized experimental protocol.

The following information is not yet available as a complete dataset:

- Identical prompts tested systematically across models
- A consistent number of generations per condition
- Standardized scoring criteria
- Complete records of model versions and settings
- A curated collection of outputs linked to their original prompts

Consequently, these observations cannot establish that any particular generator has a stable or unique visual fingerprint.

## 7. Proposed Follow-Up Experiment

A future experiment will use the same creative brief across multiple generators.

The experiment will document the prompts, available model information, settings, outputs, and recurring visual characteristics.

The comparison will evaluate how closely each output follows the intended direction, rather than simply judging which image looks the most attractive.

## 8. Research Principle

A recurring tendency should be documented before it is explained.

Unexpected outputs are useful starting points for investigation, but conclusions require evidence that can be examined and compared.
